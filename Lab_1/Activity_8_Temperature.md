# Lab 1 — Activity 8: Understanding Temperature in LLMs

## Objective

The objective of this activity is to understand how the temperature parameter affects the output generated by a Large Language Model.

Temperature controls how randomly the model selects the next token.

## 1. Temperature 0.1

At a temperature of **0.1**, the model tends to produce more predictable and consistent responses.

The output is usually focused and less varied.

### Observation

- More predictable
- Less creative
- Similar responses for repeated prompts
- Useful when consistency is important

## 2. Temperature 0.7

At a temperature of **0.7**, the model produces a balance between predictability and creativity.

The response can have some variation while still remaining relevant to the prompt.

### Observation

- Moderate creativity
- Moderate randomness
- More varied responses
- Useful for general-purpose text generation

## 3. Temperature 1.0

At a temperature of **1.0**, the model generally allows more randomness in token selection.

The responses can become more creative and diverse.

### Observation

- More creative
- More varied
- Less predictable
- Useful for brainstorming and creative writing

## 4. Comparison

| Temperature | Predictability | Creativity | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | High | Low | Factual or consistent tasks |
| 0.7 | Medium | Medium | General conversation |
| 1.0 | Lower | High | Creative generation |

## 5. Does Temperature Change the Model's Knowledge?

No.

Temperature does **not** change the knowledge stored in the model.

It changes how the model selects among possible next tokens during generation.

Therefore, changing the temperature does not add new knowledge to the model. It mainly changes the style and variation of the generated response.

## 6. When Should Low or High Temperature Be Used?

### Low Temperature

A low temperature is useful when predictable and consistent output is preferred.

Examples:

- Classification
- Structured responses
- Information extraction
- Coding assistance

### High Temperature

A higher temperature can be useful when creativity and variety are preferred.

Examples:

- Brainstorming
- Story writing
- Creative ideas
- Generating multiple possible approaches

## Conclusion

Temperature controls the randomness of LLM generation.

A low temperature generally produces more predictable responses, while a higher temperature can produce more diverse and creative responses.

Temperature changes the generation process, not the model's underlying knowledge.
